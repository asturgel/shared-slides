People Leadership · Talent Development

Maggie — from contractor to campaign leader

+
Situation
Hired Maggie as a contractor for a content audit. Recent grad, math major working in marketing — a data nerd in a creative org. The role was about crunching numbers; she was already asking why, not just what.
Task
Help her see what I saw — and design a path that grew her into it.
Action
Offered her full-time with expanded scope (she was shocked). Built the role around her strengths and deliberate stretch areas. Used 1:1s as the debrief space — how to handle pushback, how to open meetings with a clearer point of view. Confidence isn't built in big moments — it's built in small reps with honest feedback right after.
Result
When her curiosity outgrew the team, I heard about a campaign activation manager role, advocated for her, and helped her prep. She got the role. My team lost a great person — and that was the right outcome.
"Real talent development isn't retention — it's readiness."
Executive Influence · Steering Committee

Conditional Activation Roadmap

+
Situation
Premier Customer UAP rollout — Cisco's flagship engagement platform for our most strategic accounts. Data quality issues from a related platform (UCD) were undermining seller confidence. The org's instinct was to push forward to hit the milestone.
Task
Bring the SVP and steering committee a recommendation that protected both the program and the sellers — without losing executive support.
Action
Designed the Conditional Activation Roadmap — dependency gates, contingency pivots, activation timed to data readiness rather than calendar. Came with seller sentiment data, technical risk indicators, and adoption health signals. Reframed the conversation from "How fast can we go?" to "How do we protect seller experience?"
Result
Rapid adoption and consistently positive seller sentiment across the launch. The steering committee now uses the Conditional Activation Roadmap as the go-forward plan, and the North Stars in it have become a template for activation across other programs.
Matrixed Influence · Smart Content

Gartner-recognized cross-functional transformation

+
Situation
2020 — leading the Smart Content initiative as Sr. Content Strategist. Cisco's content creation process was fragmented; creators were overwhelmed and sellers couldn't find what they needed. The challenge wasn't technical — it was organizational.
Task
Align six stakeholder groups — none of which reported to me (IT, Marketing Ops, Sales Ops, Sales Enablement, Content Creators, Sellers) — and secure sponsorship across the matrix.
Action
Built a tailored WIIFM for every persona. Started with 1:1 conversations to understand pain points before drafting the plan. The first steerco was the lightbulb moment — all six groups in one room, each seeing their needs in the broader vision. Won IT through the others, not directly. Sustained alignment through cross-functional working sessions and shared artifacts that made dependencies visible.
Result
Materially compressed content operations cycle time. Initiative still in use at Cisco today. Became a Gartner case study — external validation of the cross-functional approach.
"In a matrix, you don't move forward by mandate. You move forward by connecting the dots between what each group needs and what the customer needs."
Methodology · Behavior Over Buttons

ADKAR diagnosis on UAP — desire, not awareness

+
Situation
UAP — AI-powered, Salesforce-embedded engagement model deployed at global scale. The temptation in any tool launch is to train the clicks, drive logins, and measure activity. That approach delivers short-term metrics and long-term failure.
Task
Drive adoption that translated into actual behavior change — and earn executive alignment around shifting from a tool-centric to a behavior-centric strategy.
Action
Used ADKAR as the diagnostic spine. Awareness was high; Desire was the real friction. Built persona-based impact assessments — AEs convene the team, SEs translate not just present, CS in Phase 1 not Phase 3, Sales Leaders coach not just inspect. Engaged managers as the front line of behavior change with toolkits and 1:1 prompts before launching IC training. Reframed the executive question from "How do we drive tool adoption faster?" to "How do we drive the right behaviors that the tool enables?"
Result
Rapid adoption and consistently positive seller sentiment. High intentional engagement at the tool surface — strong indicators of intentional, not accidental, use. The persona-based, behavior-led approach is now the activation playbook for adjacent SSPO programs.
Self-Awareness · Hardest Lesson

What I'd do differently — sponsorship as the foundation

+
Situation
Leading a strategic operating-model effort whose impact extended well beyond my direct team. The work needed executive sponsorship to land.
Task
Land the operating-model recommendation across the organization.
Action
I invested deeply in the strategic and analytical work and built a well-evidenced recommendation — but underinvested in sponsorship. Without strong executive sponsorship, every conversation became persuasion from below. When priorities shifted, the work didn't have the protection it needed; my team was impacted by a restructuring. That outcome stayed with me.
Result
The strategy was right. The lesson wasn't about strategy — it was about sponsorship as a foundation, not a finish line. In my current role, sponsorship is the first thing I architect, not the last. The SVP and steering committee engagement that made the Conditional Activation Roadmap possible exists because of this lesson — not in spite of it.
"Great strategy without sponsorship is fragile. As a leader, my responsibility isn't just to the work — it's to the people doing the work alongside me."
Team Building · Culture That Holds

"The heart is not in the cooler" — leading a team through change

+
Situation

Director-Level POV

Where this team should go next.

The current SSPO change function is organized around the tools we deliver. Sellers don't experience their job as a series of tools — they experience it as a workflow. Structure shapes output. To deliver the connected workflow story we're committing to externally, we need to organize ourselves internally around workflows.

Today · Tool-Organized

Activation by tool

Each tool — Gong, UAP, ExecConsole — has dedicated activation. Strong individual launches; fragmented seller experience.

  • Mirrors the tool catalog, not the workflow
  • Connected story is hard to land in market
  • New tool = new team motion
→
Proposed · Workflow-Led

Workflow Leaders + Tool SME Overlay

Two-tier model. Workflow leaders own the seller experience and behavior change. Tool SMEs are the technical overlay that plugs into multiple workflows.

  • Connected Strategy — UAP · ExecConsole · AI Sales Assist
  • Connected Engagement — Gong · forecasting · AI Sales Assist
  • Connected Execution — CS Console · UAP · CS platforms
Transition approach. Sequence the change — don't restructure during peak delivery. Pilot the model with one workflow (Connected Strategy is the natural starter, since UAP is already at the center of it), validate the operating rhythm in parallel, then scale. Reinforce with shared metrics that measure workflow outcomes — not just tool adoption.

  • Surface 1–2 quick wins for the team to deliver visibly.
  • Align cross-functional governance.
Days 61 – 90

Set Direction

  • Publish a team strategy aligned to SSPO priorities.
  • Establish the KPI framework for measuring change-initiative success.
  • Identify capability gaps; propose hiring & development plan.
  • Begin executive cadence with SVP and steering committee.
